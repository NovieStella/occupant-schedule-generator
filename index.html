Occupant Schedule Generator

Turns annual work-plane illuminance into stochastic occupant lighting schedules using published behaviour models, and compares them with a fixed standard schedule.

Version 1.0 · method, sources and limitations

    1. Illuminance data

    CSV with one column of work-plane illuminance in lux, 8760 rows (one per hour, non-leap year). One optional header row is fine.

    The file is read in your browser. Nothing is uploaded anywhere.

    2. Presence profile

    Weekday presence probability for each hour, 24 values from 00:00 to 23:00, each 0–1. Paste a row or column from Excel. Weekends are absent in v1.

    Default: Sveby (2010), Brukarindata kontor: P = 0.70 on weekdays 08:00–17:00 (9 hours), 0 otherwise.

    3. Mobility μ hourly time step, not from paper

    Page et al. (2008) mobility parameter, Eq. (4). The paper used a 15-minute step and gives no hourly values, so μ here is your input.

    "≈ 15-min μ" is the 15-minute μ with the same hour-to-hour persistence where P is constant (two-state Markov chain, state read on the hour). Orientation only. Defaults correspond to 15-minute μ = 0.10, 0.20 and 0.40.

    4. Lighting switching

    Lightswitch-2002 (Reinhart 2004) without blinds, for private or two-person offices. Needs the illuminance CSV; the sensor value stands in for the minimum work-plane illuminance.

    Model inputs from the papers
    Arrival, Dd: Hunt (1979) Fig. 9(d)P = Φ(−2.58·(log₁₀E − 1.82))
    Arrival, Di: Reinhart (2004) Fig. 3(a)P = 1
    While present, per 5 min: Reinhart Fig. 3(b)0.0027 + 0.017 / (1 + e64.19·(log₁₀E − 2.41))
    While present, per hour1 − (1 − P₅)¹²

    Switch-off at departure: Reinhart (2004) Fig. 3(c), "no controls", by length of the coming absence. No switch-off while present.

    5. Run settings

    Must match "Day of Week for Start Day" in the IDF RunPeriod. Default Wednesday, from Stella's simulation.

    6. Energy and comparison

    Updates instantly, no re-run needed. kWh/m²·yr = lit hours × LPD (W/m²) ÷ 1000.

    The same LPD is used for all three cases, so the comparison isolates behaviour. Fixed case: Sveby (2010) ch. 8, lights on 100 % on weekdays 08:00–17:00 regardless of daylight or presence.

    7. Export

    Per occupant: lighting (0/1) and occupancy (0/1), 8760 rows, one column per occupant, one file per μ. For EnergyPlus/Honeybee Schedule:File, IES VE and IDA ICE. Includes summaries and a metadata file with every coefficient, setting and assumption.

    Method, sources and limitations

    Version 1.0. Behaviour comes only from published, cited models running on one seeded random number generator: the same inputs and seed give identical results. AI was used to build the tool, not to invent behaviour.

    1. What the tool compares

    Four cases for one office zone, all on/off lighting with the same lighting power density (LPD), so the comparison isolates behaviour. Annual lighting energy (kWh/m²·yr) = lit hours × LPD (W/m²) ÷ 1000.

    1. Fixed schedule (Sveby 2010, ch. 8): lights on 100 % on weekdays 08:00–17:00, regardless of daylight or presence.
    2. Deterministic threshold: the same window with full occupancy, lights on when the work-plane illuminance is below the setpoint (500 lx, EN 12464-1; 300 lx option).
    3. Ideal switching: lights on exactly when the stochastic occupant is present and the illuminance is below the setpoint.
    4. Stochastic: stochastic presence and stochastic manual switching (below).

    Read in this order, the steps separate a daylight effect (1→2), a presence effect (2→3) and a behaviour effect (3→4). The steps are sequential: a different order would split the interactions differently.

    2. Presence model

    Inhomogeneous Markov chain of Page et al. (2008), Eq. (2), (4), (5), (6), driven by a 24-hour weekday presence profile (default Sveby 2010, Brukarindata kontor: P = 0.70 from 08:00 to 17:00) and a mobility parameter μ. When the input μ would give a transition probability outside 0–1, μ is moved to the closest valid value for that hour step, as in the paper, and the tool logs where. At P(t) = 0 or 1 the transition out of a state that cannot occur is set to P(t+1) (assumption; the paper does not cover it). Occupants start absent at 00:00 on 1 January; weekends are absent.

    Time step and μ. The tool runs hourly; the paper used 15 minutes and gives no numeric μ values. μ is therefore a user input labelled "hourly time step, not from paper", run as a sensitivity (defaults 0.38, 0.67, 0.93, equal in hour-to-hour persistence to 15-minute μ of 0.10, 0.20, 0.40 where P is constant).

    3. Lighting switching model

    Lightswitch-2002 algorithm of Reinhart (2004, section 2.1, Fig. 2, Table 1) without blinds, evaluated hourly:

    • Arrival with lights off: users who consider daylight (Dd) switch on with Hunt's (1979) probit line, P = Φ(−2.58·(log₁₀E − 1.82)), section 5.2.4 and Fig. 9(d) (n = 213, r = −0.82), on the minimum work-plane illuminance; users who ignore daylight (Di) always switch on. Φ uses the error-function approximation of Abramowitz & Stegun (1964), formula 7.1.26.
    • While present with lights off: intermediate switch-on, Reinhart (2004) Fig. 3(b). The printed formula is garbled; the tool uses P₅ = 0.0027 + 0.017 / (1 + e64.19·(log₁₀E − 2.41)) per 5 minutes, which reproduces the plotted curve, converted to hourly as 1 − (1 − P₅)¹².
    • No switch-off while present (section 2.4).
    • Departure with lights on: switch off with Reinhart (2004) Fig. 3(c), "no controls" (from Pigg et al. 1996), by the length of the coming absence, which the presence series provides. Lights not switched off stay on through the absence.

    The Dd/Di share is an input (default 50 %, Reinhart's equal shares); real buildings are often polarised.

    4. Time convention

    All files are in standard time (UTC+1), including the illuminance. During EU summer time (last Sunday of March to last Sunday of October) office hours follow the clock, so the presence profile and the Sveby window move one hour earlier in standard time (08:00–17:00 clock = 07:00–16:00 standard). Reinhart (2004, section 3.1) also applied daylight saving time to occupancy. In EnergyPlus, use Adjust Schedule for Daylight Savings = No.

    5. Case study: Reinhart reference office in Malmö

    Daylight input. Annual hourly illuminance simulated with honeybee-radiance and Radiance 6.0a for the Reinhart (2004) reference office (section 3.1, Fig. 5): 3.05 × 6.1 × 2.74 m, facing south, no obstructions; reflectances 80/50/20 % (ceiling/walls/floor); double low-e glazing, visual transmittance 72 %; window digitised from Fig. 5(a) (4.58 m² of glass); no blinds, no furniture. Malmö TMYx weather. Two-phase daylight coefficients with an exact sun, Perez sky (Perez et al. 1993). Sensor on the room centre line, 0.8 m high, 4.5 m from the window: daylit mean 697 lx, daylight factor 1.4 %.

    Daylight-factor check. At 1.5 m from the window the model gives 8.5 %, against 11 % in Reinhart (2004). Ruled out: the glass (Radiance transmits exactly 0.72 at normal incidence) and convergence (8.55–8.58 % from 6 to 12 ambient bounces; an independent rtrace run gives 8.5 %). Only a frameless pane from sill to ceiling reaches 11 %, which contradicts Fig. 5(a). The window is kept as drawn and the gap is reported.

    Result (100 occupants, seed 12345, 50 % Dd, μ = 0.38, 500 lx, 10 W/m², 1 January a Wednesday, summer time on):

    kWh/m²·yrFixedDaylight effectPresence effectBehaviour effectStochastic
    Dd users23.5−14.8−2.6+0.16.1
    Di users23.5−14.8−2.7+12.218.2

    Finding. At a realistic desk, occupants who use daylight behave close to ideal switching (6.1 against 6.0 kWh/m²·yr), and nearly the whole behaviour effect (99 %) comes from occupants who ignore daylight. For Dd users "close to ideal" is a net result of two opposite effects: lights left on through some absences and never switched off while present (more energy), and dim hours tolerated without switching on (less energy). At μ = 0.67 and 0.93 the second wins and Dd users use slightly less than ideal switching (5.9 and 5.8). The Dd/Di share therefore matters more than the mean: report the two groups, not the average of both.

    Outliers are genuine. Lights left on at a departure stay on until a later departure where they are switched off. Before an absence of 12 h or more the switch-off probability is 0.990, so about 1 in 100 departures made with the lights on before a night or weekend leaves them on, for up to 63 h over a weekend. In the case study 33 of 100 occupants do this at least once a year, and the occupant with the most lit-while-absent hours has 354 h (144 on weekends). These rare, long episodes form the upper tail of the distribution.

    Plausibility, not validation. Reinhart's DdBd user in Toronto used 9.6 kWh/m²·yr at 15 W/m² (Eq. 1), about 6.4 at 10 W/m², against 6.1 here (different climate, presence and blinds). Measured lighting use in Swedish offices of about 65 % of the time, dropping toward 30 % in summer (Jagemar 2004, in Sveby Bilaga 2), is far below the fixed schedule's 100 %.

    6. Limitations
    • Private or two-person offices only (Reinhart 2004); not open-plan offices.
    • Hourly time step: breaks under one hour are invisible, so the two shortest switch-off bins are never used; the hourly intermediate probability assumes constant illuminance within the hour.
    • μ is a sensitivity input, not a value from the paper.
    • Hunt's (1979) data came from UK multi-person offices and classrooms, with illuminance estimated from external data and daylight factors; Reinhart (2004) adopted the curve for private offices. Hunt writes "less than 1 %" at 500 lux; the probit equation gives 1.2 %.
    • Reinhart (2004) Fig. 3(b): Table 1 says the intermediate probability rises "from 0.5 % to 2 %"; the formula and plot give 0.27 % to 1.97 %.
    • One sensor point stands in for the minimum work-plane illuminance.
    • No blinds, no long absences (holidays, sick leave) and no public holidays. The Swedish July holiday falls in high-daylight months, so its effect on lighting energy is small.
    • Each stochastic occupant is treated as controlling the lighting of the whole zone; on/off lighting without dimming.
    • Daylight input is simulated, not measured (section 5). Schedule import is tested in EnergyPlus 25.2.0 (exact match, with and without daylight saving in the model); Honeybee, IES VE and IDA ICE are expected to work but not yet tested.
